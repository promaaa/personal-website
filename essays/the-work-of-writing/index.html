The Work of Writing

Why I keep writing when AI can do it for me: understanding my own ideas, finding their gaps, and keeping a record of how they change.

The Work of Writing

Writing is something we are first made to do throughout school. Stories, essays, text analyses, mathematical proofs, physics problems. It is slow, sometimes tedious, and it takes concentration.

So why keep doing it? Today, I can connect ChatGPT to my inbox, ask Claude to write an assignment, and have Gemini explain something again. Producing text is becoming easy enough to skip much of the work.

And yet, more than ever, I think we need to write. Emails, messages, essays, our own reflections. A lot, even if we get a little obsessive about it.

Just try explaining an idea you think you understand. The first few sentences come easily enough, then you get stuck on a connection you cannot justify. You look for an example and discover it does not really work. What seemed obvious in your head suddenly becomes much less clear.

That is exactly the work I am interested in. Writing exposes the gaps in my reasoning. I have to choose my words, make clear what I am claiming, and sometimes admit that I do not quite know what I think.

AI can help me keep working through this. It can also finish the job for me, with a result convincing enough that I no longer feel the need to revisit it. The text might be better. The question is how much I will have understood while producing it.

Writing also lets us keep a record of what we thought at a particular moment. A few months later, we can read it again and find ourselves naive, excessive, or still fairly reasonable. At least we can see what has changed.

And finally, I enjoy it. Some thoughts stop going round in circles once they are on a page. A worry becomes more specific; a problem starts to feel manageable. Sometimes I simply understand why something has been on my mind so much.

So I will keep writing for as long as I can. Even slowly, even clumsily, even when a tool could do it better. I still need to go through that process to know what I think.

Marc Duboc
